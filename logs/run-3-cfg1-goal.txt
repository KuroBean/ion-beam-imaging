Run 3, chamber setup 1 (tube alone), high collection: find the finest worst-case dtheta while collecting as much of the beam as possible. eta_min is 0.6: with the 0.7 mesh transmission that means at least ~86% of the emitted weight on the active MCP (meniscus angles out to about 32 deg), without the mesh 60% (about 23-24 deg); collecting more than the minimum is welcome when it costs little resolution. The tip-E1 extraction voltage may be chosen between 120 and 300 V.

New in cfg 1: the MCP is the last part on the lens stack's tie rods. z_mcp is a variable: anywhere behind the last present part (or behind the screen mesh, which the MCP carries at mesh_gap) up to 130.7 mm behind the E1 front face. There are no deflectors. Removing plates or the screen mesh lets the MCP come closer to the stack, so the throw length (stack to MCP) is a new lever on the magnification and on where the MCP field acts.

An idea from the author, to weigh rather than follow: the last plates in front of the MCP could shield the beam from the MCP's strong negative potential (the job the screen mesh does now), so the MCP field no longer reaches far upstream as an uncontrolled lens. The author pictures the best compromise between resolution and collection as a beam that expands out to fill the bore of the last electrode and is then projected almost directly onto the MCP, which can now sit close behind that electrode. Check this against the numbers: the plate bores are 5 mm in radius and the MCP active radius is 20 mm, and resolution is detector limited (dtheta = sigma_MCP / (dr/dtheta)), so the beam still has to spread between the last plate and the MCP to use the full MCP.

What run 2 taught (cfg 3, eta_min 0.6, extraction 120-300 V, 700 solves):
- Best 0.0879 deg at eta 0.644: tip 198 V, E1 +32 V (extraction 166 V), Einzel E2-E3-E4 with E3 positive (+166 V) and E2/E4 strongly negative (-248 V), E5-E9 and both deflectors removed, no screen mesh, MCP front -6.9 kV, lens_gap 20.6 mm.
- Resolution is detector limited: dtheta = sigma_MCP / (dr/dtheta). For a linear map the worst dtheta is about sigma_MCP x theta_max / R_MCP, and eta_min sets theta_max. Without the mesh eta 0.6 needed meniscus angles out to about 23-24 deg (floor ~0.082 deg); with the 0.7 mesh about 32 deg (floor ~0.11 deg). The best runs came close to these floors. What remained was linearity: r(theta) was convex, so the worst dtheta sat at theta = 0, where the near-axis slope was smallest.
- Without the mesh, the MCP field reaches far upstream (about 150 mm in cfg 3) and acts as the final converging lens.
- What did not work: a second Einzel E7-E8-E9 sharing the first one's channels (over-focus, two crossovers, eta 0.06); moving E3 and E4 back (retarding regime, ions near stall, eta 0.01); moving E4 alone (no gain); grounding E1 to free a channel for an asymmetric E2/E3/E4 lens (0.142 deg); E7 as an extra field plate (0.228 deg, map more convex); a spread lens E2/E5/E7 (0.372 deg, clipping on E1). Lost ions ended mostly on the upstream face of E1 and on the 5 mm E1-E4 bores.
- Round 16 probed cfg 1 once, with the MCP then fixed at 145 mm and no screen mesh (Einzel E2-E3-E4, E5-E9 removed): 0.216 deg at eta 0.867 after 25 solves, worst at theta = 0 (convex map), never refined. Wall loss was about 1% there, against 17-25% in cfg 3, where the beam swings out in the long drift.
